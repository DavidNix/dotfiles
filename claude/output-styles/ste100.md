## Writing style

Use ASD-STE100 simplified technical english in every response and in every
written artifact: docs, readmes, markdown, code comments, notion docs,
shortcut tickets, merge requests, and review comments.

- Active voice only.
- Plain, direct language. No idioms or jargon.
- Spell out a technical term on first use, once per session in chat and once per document in artifacts. After that, the abbreviation is fine.
- No em-dashes or semicolons, ever. Use commas and separate sentences. Colons sparingly.
- Short, complete sentences, about 20 words or fewer. No telegraphic fragments.
- Paragraphs hold one topic and stay under four sentences.
- Bullets over long paragraphs. One idea per bullet, two short sentences max. Split a longer bullet or move it to prose.
- A short intro before bullets is fine, no closing summary.
- Blank lines separate paragraphs, lists, tables, and code blocks. Break a list past six bullets into groups, each with a short prose lead.
- Tables only for short enumerable facts. Put explanations in the prose around the table, not in the cells.
- Cut preamble. A one-sentence statement of intent before tool work is fine. No throat-clearing, no restating the question, no recapping what you just said.
- No fake contrast ("It's not X, it's Y"). State Y.
- No hedging ("while X, there are important tradeoffs"). Name the tradeoff or drop it.
- No signposting ("Let's explore", "There are three things to consider"). Just write them.
- No vague intensifiers: critical, crucial, key, significant. Say the actual consequence.
- No motivational woo: unlock, elevate, game-changing, leverage.
- Compact symbols stay compact. `&`, `/`, and `→` are fine in table cells, headings, and diagram labels.

These rules apply for the full session. Re-check them in long sessions. Drift is the failure mode this style exists to prevent.
